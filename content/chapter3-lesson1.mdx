Lesson 1 of Chapter 3 opens the handbook's treatment of **electromagnetism** (អគ្គិសនីនិងម៉ាញេទិច) with a full account of **magnetism and magnetic forces** (ដែននិងកម្លាំងម៉ាញេទិក). Beginning with natural and artificial magnets, the lesson derives the magnetic field produced by current-carrying conductors of different geometries, establishes the Lorentz force law, traces the circular path of a charged particle in a magnetic field, and finishes with two precision measurement applications: magnetic deflection and the mass spectrograph. Equations (១) through (១២) are defined in this lesson.

## 1. Natural and Artificial Magnets (មេដែក)

A **magnet** (មេដែក) is any object that can attract iron. Every magnet has two inseparable poles: a **north pole (N)** and a **south pole (S)**. The interaction rule is:

- Like poles (**N–N** or **S–S**) repel each other.
- Unlike poles (**N–S**) attract each other.

**Natural magnets** exist in nature — the primary example is magnetite (lodestone), Fe₃O₄. **Artificial magnets** are manufactured to specific shapes: bar magnets, needle magnets, and U-shaped magnets.

## 2. Earth's Magnetic Field (ដែនម៉ាញេទិចផែនដី)

Earth behaves as a giant magnet. Two important angle concepts:

| Quantity | Khmer term | Definition |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Magnetic declination** | ឌិចក្លីណេស្យុងម៉ាញេទិក | Angle between magnetic north and geographic north |
| **Magnetic inclination** | អាំងក្លីណេស្យុងម៉ាញេទិក | Angle between field line direction and the horizontal plane |

<Note>
Earth's geographic north pole is actually near a magnetic south pole — that is why the north-seeking end of a compass needle points toward it. Declination varies by location and changes slowly over time.
</Note>

## 3. Magnetic Field from Current-Carrying Conductors

### 3.1 Straight Wire — Equation (១)

A long straight wire carrying current $I$, at perpendicular distance $r$ from the wire, produces magnetic field:

$$\boxed{B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}} \tag{១}$$

**Right-hand grip rule:** Wrap the right hand around the wire with the thumb pointing in the direction of current $I$; the curling fingers show the direction of the circular field lines.

**Constants:** $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\ \text{T·m/A}$ (permeability of free space).

### 3.2 Circular Current Loop — Equation (២)

At the centre of a single circular loop of radius $R$ carrying current $I$:

$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2R} \tag{២}$$

### 3.3 Solenoid — Equation (៣)

A solenoid with $n$ turns per unit length carrying current $I$ produces a uniform interior field:

$$\boxed{B = \mu_0 n I} \tag{៣}$$

where $n = N/l$ (total turns $N$ divided by length $l$).

<Tip>
For a solenoid, the field inside is nearly perfectly uniform and the field outside is nearly zero — making it the ideal electromagnet geometry for laboratory applications.
</Tip>

## 4. Lorentz Force on a Moving Charge

### 4.1 Magnetic Force — Equation (៤)

A charge $q$ moving at speed $v$ perpendicular to a magnetic field $B$ experiences:

$$\boxed{F_B = |q|vB} \tag{៤}$$

The force direction is given by the **right-hand rule** for cross products: $\vec{F}_B = q\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$.

### 4.2 Circular Trajectory Radius — Equation (៥)

Because $F_B$ is always perpendicular to $v$, it acts as centripetal force. Setting $F_B = mv^2/R$:

$$|q|vB = \frac{mv^2}{R}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\boxed{R = \frac{mv}{|q|B}} \tag{៥}$$

**TikZ figure in source:** Diagram of a positive charge entering a magnetic field region and curving into a circular arc, with radius $R$, velocity $v$, and force $F_B$ labelled.

### 4.3 Helix Trajectory

If the velocity has a component parallel to $\vec{B}$, the particle follows a **helix**: circular motion in the plane perpendicular to $\vec{B}$, combined with uniform motion along $\vec{B}$.

## 5. Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

### 5.1 Force on a Straight Wire — Equation (៦)

A wire of length $L$ carrying current $I$ in a uniform field $B$ (field perpendicular to wire) experiences:

$$\boxed{F_B = BIL} \tag{៦}$$

### 5.2 Force Between Two Parallel Wires — Equation (៧)

Two parallel wires separated by distance $d$, carrying currents $I_a$ and $I_b$ over length $L$, exert a mutual force:

$$\boxed{F = \frac{\mu_0 I_a I_b L}{2\pi d}} \tag{៧}$$

- Currents in the **same direction** → wires **attract**.
- Currents in **opposite directions** → wires **repel**.

**TikZ figures in source:** Side-by-side diagrams showing the two cases (attraction and repulsion) with field lines from each wire and force arrows on each conductor.

## 6. Rotating-Frame Motor Application

A rectangular current loop in a uniform field experiences a **torque** that tends to align the loop with the field. This is the operating principle of a DC motor. When the loop plane is parallel to $\vec{B}$, the torque is maximum; when perpendicular, it is zero (stable equilibrium). A **commutator** reverses the current direction every half-turn to maintain continuous rotation.

## 7. Magnetic Deflection of Charged Particles

### 7.1 Deflection Angle — Equation (৮)

A charged particle of mass $m$, charge $q$, initial speed $v$ entering a magnetic field region of length $l$ is deflected by a small angle:

$$\boxed{\alpha = \frac{|q|Bl}{mv}} \tag{৮}$$

## 8. Mass Spectrograph — Equations (৯)–(১২)

The mass spectrograph separates ions by mass-to-charge ratio. An ion accelerated through potential difference $V$ gains kinetic energy:

$$\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = qV \tag{৯}$$

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{2qV}{m}} \tag{১০}$$

The ion then enters a magnetic field $B$ and follows a circular arc of radius $R$:

$$R = \frac{mv}{qB} \tag{১১}$$

Eliminating $v$ between equations (১০) and (১১) gives the **mass spectrograph formula**:

$$\boxed{m = \frac{q(RB)^2}{2V}} \tag{১২}$$

<Accordion title="Derivation detail">

From $R = mv/qB$ we get $v = qBR/m$. Substituting into the energy equation $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = qV$:

$$\frac{1}{2}m\left(\frac{qBR}{m}\right)^2 = qV
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\frac{q^2B^2R^2}{2m} = qV
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
m = \frac{q(RB)^2}{2V}$$

By measuring the radius $R$ of the ion arc (from the impact position on a photographic plate), the mass $m$ can be determined precisely.

</Accordion>
